# **Colorado**

## **📰 News**

- ​Voter Registration
  - As always make sure your family and friends are [registered](https://sos.state.co.us/voter/pages/pub/home.xhtml)!
- Mail Ballot Fight
  - Colorado has joined a [lawsuit](https://www.cpr.org/2026/08/26/colorado-lawsiut-postal-service-mail-ballots/) against the U.S. Postal Service over new Trump administration mail ballot restrictions. The fight is significant because Colorado law requires ballots to be mailed to all eligible voters, and Colorado officials argue the federal government is attempting to interfere with state-run elections.
- Mail Ballot ID Measure Qualifies
  - Colorado voters will [decide](https://coloradosun.com/2026/08/26/mail-ballot-id-colorado-initiative/) whether mail ballots should require the last four digits of a Social Security number, driver’s license number, or state ID number in addition to the state’s current signature verification process.
  - Supporters argue the measure strengthens election security, while opponents argue Colorado’s signature verification system is already secure and that the additional ID requirement could lead to more ballots being rejected. Because the measure would amend the state constitution, it needs 55% support to pass.
- 3rd Congressional District Tightens
  - Republican Rep. [Jeff Hurd](https://coloradosun.com/2026/08/27/jeff-hurd-donald-trump-2026-election-colorado/) is highlighting votes where he broke from President Trump as Colorado’s 3rd Congressional District appears more competitive heading into November. Hurd has pointed to votes on health insurance subsidies, tariffs, and a southeastern Colorado water bill as evidence that he is willing to split from the national party when needed.
  - The move shows how Trump remains a liability in competitive Colorado races. Hurd is still a Republican incumbent in a Republican-leaning district, but his campaign is clearly trying to present him as more independent than the rest of the Colorado Republican delegation.
- Denver Water Restrictions
  - [Denver Water](https://coloradosun.com/2026/08/26/denver-water-approves-oct-1-ban-on-lawn-watering/) approved an October 1 cutoff for lawn watering for its 1.5 million customers, with warnings and potential fines for sprinkler use after that date. Limited hand watering for trees, shrubs, and perennials will still be allowed.
  - The restriction comes as Denver’s reservoirs sit well below normal levels for this time of year. The drought is moving from background concern into daily life, with water limits now affecting households across the metro area.
