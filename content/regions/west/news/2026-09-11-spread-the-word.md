# **Colorado**

## **📰 News**

- Victor Marx Refuses Debate with Weiser and Lopez
  - Republican gubernatorial nominee Victor Marx has [declined](https://www.cpr.org/2026/08/10/colorado-governor-candidate-debates) traditional moderated debates, including a 9News invitation, saying he will participate in one direct, candidate-to-candidate discussion without a moderator controlling the questions or topics.
  - Phil Weiser has rejected Marx’s proposed format and already debated unaffiliated candidate Greg Lopez. The two major-party campaigns still have not agreed to an in-person debate. 
- 14 Measures on November Ballot Tie Record 
  - Thirteen of Colorado’s [14 statewide ballot questions](https://coloradonewsline.com/2026/09/08/14-measures-will-appear-on-colorado) came through citizen initiatives, with only one measure referred by the state legislature. The total ties the record for the most statewide measures on a Colorado ballot in at least 52 years.
  - Voters will decide fights over a graduated income tax, immigration enforcement, fentanyl penalties, transgender policy, voter ID, natural gas, hunting and fishing rights, congressional redistricting and TABOR.
- Johnston Launches Reelection Campaign
  - [Denver Mayor Mike Johnston](https://denverite.com/2026/09/08/denver-mayor-mike-johnston-reelection-campaign-kickoff) officially launched his reelection campaign Tuesday, running on reductions in street homelessness and crime, lower rents and a recovering downtown while arguing that his first-term agenda needs four more years.
  - Johnston is promising another 20,000 affordable homes, universal child care and continued reductions in homelessness as he heads into a race that already includes nine challengers. 
- ICE Expands as Construction Labor Tightens
  - GEO Group is moving forward with plans for a roughly 1,200-bed ICE detention facility in [Hudson](https://www.cpr.org/2026/07/13/ice-detention-center-hudson-confirmed), which would become Colorado’s second major immigration detention center alongside the existing Aurora facility.
  - At the same time, Colorado contractors say increased immigration enforcement is adding to an existing [skilled-labor shortage](https://www.cpr.org/2026/09/03/colorado-construction-jobs-competition). Fourteen percent of Colorado respondents in a recent construction workforce survey reported an immigration-enforcement visit to a jobsite or offsite location within the previous six months.
- Colorado River Nears a Legal Tripwire
  - Colorado River flows at Lee’s Ferry could fall below a [critical 10-year threshold](https://www.cpr.org/2026/09/09/colorado-river-supreme-court-stakes) as soon as October, potentially giving Lower Basin states grounds to argue that the Upper Basin is violating its obligations under the 1922 Colorado River Compact.
  - A Supreme Court fight could put Colorado cities, Western Slope communities, farms and other post-1922 water users at risk of mandatory cuts. Colorado is already preparing for possible litigation while negotiations between the seven basin states remain unresolved.

This might be true, but there has been an increase in rejected ballots, from around [16,000 in the 2024 primary to around 20,000 in the 2026 primary](https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2026/09/02/uncounted-late-ballots-jump-in-wa-as-fears-of-postal-service-delays-intensify/).
