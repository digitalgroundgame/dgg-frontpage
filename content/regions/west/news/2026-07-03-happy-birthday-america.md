Quick disclaimer but been busy this week so the general server updates are a bit lighter this week. Fortunately our state news contributors have been on top of things and have their usual fill of updates for you all to read heading into this holiday weekend.

On that note, as America approaches its 250th birthday, I just want to highlight the World Cup again for giving us all these stories and images of people from all over the world coming together in celebration of the tournament and sharing in their excitement for the games across the country (and Canada and Mexico!).

Whether it's the people of Lawrence, Kansas warmly adopting the Algerian national team as their own, Bostonians teaching the Scottish about baseball and to hate on the Yankees, or Mexican and Korean fans becoming bros in LA, I think it presents a version of America that is welcoming, multicultural, and optimistic.

Of course that stands in stark contrast to the awfulness of this administration, but it's good to be reminded of the good parts of this country and why so many people from around the world still want to come here.

That positive vision is exactly what we're working to live up to with this org so as we celebrate the 4th of July, let's keep that image in mind to use as fuel for organizing as we head into the midterms 🇺🇸

# **Colorado**

## **📰  News**

**July 2 Colorado Primary Roundup**

Colorado’s June 30 primary delivered one of the clearest anti-establishment signals in recent state Democratic politics. Progressive candidates scored major wins in Denver’s 1st Congressional District, Colorado’s competitive 8th Congressional District, and several statewide and down-ballot contests. At the same time, U.S. Sen. John Hickenlooper survived his own primary challenge, while the Republican primary for governor remains unresolved. 

**1st Congressional District: Newcomer Kiros defeats DeGette**

DSA-backed candidate Melat Kiros defeated nearly 30-year incumbent Diana DeGette in the Democratic primary for Colorado’s 1st Congressional District, which includes Denver. The Associated Press called the race at 10:03 p.m. Tuesday, with Kiros leading DeGette by about six percentage points.

Kiros, a 29-year-old democratic socialist and former attorney, framed her campaign as a challenge to the Democratic establishment and a call for more confrontational opposition to the Trump Administration. Her campaign emphasized Medicare for All, abolishing ICE, rejecting corporate PAC money, and opposing U.S. support to Israel. DeGette, who is a member of the Congressional Progressive Caucus, supports Medicare for All and abolishing ICE, but the two candidates differed on Israel policy - DeGette has supported restricting U.S. weapons support to defensive aid.

DeGette was first elected in 1996 and had built seniority in Congress, including a prominent role on health policy and participation in Donald Trump’s impeachment proceedings.

DeGette conceded Wednesday, congratulated Kiros, and wished her luck. As of writing no formal endorsement beyond that concession and congratulatory statement has been given. 

Colorado's 1st Congressional District remains a heavily Democratic district that has not elected a Republican since 1970.

**8th Congressional District: Rutinel defeats Bird, will face Evans**

Progressive candidate Manny Rutinel defeated former state Rep. Shannon Bird in the Democratic primary for Colorado’s 8th Congressional District. The Associated Press called the race at 7:40 p.m. Tuesday, when Rutinel was leading by about 26 percentage points.

Rutinel’s victory gives Democrats a progressive nominee in one of the country’s most closely watched House districts. Colorado’s 8th District was created after the 2020 census and first elected a representative in 2022. It stretches from Denver’s northern suburbs to Greeley and has one of the state’s largest Latino electorates.

The district has been highly competitive from the start. Democrat Yadira Caraveo narrowly won the seat in 2022, and Republican Gabe Evans flipped it in 2024 by roughly 2,000 votes. Evans now enters the general election as the Republican incumbent, while Rutinel will test whether the progressive energy seen in Denver can translate to a swing district that has repeatedly been decided by narrow margins.

At his watch party, Rutinel told supporters, “Together we will flip this seat.” Bird congratulated Rutinel and urged Democrats to unite for the general election.

**Democratic Governor Primary: Weiser defeats Bennet**

Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser defeated U.S. Sen. Michael Bennet in the Democratic primary for governor. The Associated Press called the race at 7:55 p.m. Tuesday, with Weiser leading by about 10 points.

Bennet entered the race with significant name recognition and was initially viewed as a favorite. He has represented Colorado in the U.S. Senate since 2009 and remains in that seat, with his current term running through 2028.

Weiser’s campaign focused heavily on his record as attorney general, including lawsuits filed against the Trump Administration, and argued that Colorado needed a governor rooted in state-level fights rather than Washington politics. Axios reported that Weiser had filed more than 65 lawsuits against the Trump Administration, and Reuters reported that he overcame more than $11 million in outside spending that favored Bennet.

In his victory speech, Weiser framed the result as a statement about democracy and state-level leadership, saying Colorado’s future should not be decided by out-of-state billionaires and that government should work for all Coloradans, not only the wealthy few. Bennet conceded shortly after the race was called.

Weiser will face the eventual Republican nominee in November. Gov. Jared Polis is term-limited and cannot seek another term.

**Democratic Attorney General Primary: Griswold wins four-way race**

Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold won the four-way Democratic primary for attorney general, defeating Boulder County District Attorney Michael Dougherty, civil rights attorney David Seligman, and former federal prosecutor Hetal Doshi. The Associated Press called the race with Griswold leading by about 26 points.

Griswold’s statewide name recognition as secretary of state appears to have been a major advantage in a crowded field. Axios reported the race at 45.8% for Griswold, 19.8% for Dougherty, 17.8% for Seligman, and 16.5% for Doshi at the time of its report.

Her campaign faced criticism from opponents over her courtroom experience, the accidental posting of voting system passwords during her time as secretary of state, and her decision to skip some candidate forums. Still, she won decisively and will now face Republican nominee Michael Allen in the general election.

**Republican Governor Primary: Marx-Kirkmeyer race remains uncalled**

The Republican gubernatorial primary remains too close to call between Victor Marx, a Marine veteran and evangelical ministry leader, and Barbara Kirkmeyer, a state senator and former Weld County commissioner. State Rep. Scott Bottoms is running a distant third.

As of the latest cited updates, Marx had moved into a narrow lead over Kirkmeyer. CPR reported the two were separated by 2,001 votes out of 513,201 counted, with Marx at 39.84%, Kirkmeyer at 39.45%, and Bottoms at 20.71%.

The race could remain unresolved for days. The final margin may determine whether the race falls within the state’s automatic recount threshold. Under Colorado law, an automatic recount is triggered if the margin is no more than one-half of one percent of the leading candidate’s vote total.

The winner will face Democratic nominee Phil Weiser in November. Republicans have not won a statewide race in Colorado since 2016, and the last Republican governor of Colorado was Bill Owens, who served from 1999 to 2007.

**Hickenlooper survives progressive challenge**

U.S. Sen. John Hickenlooper defeated progressive challenger Julie Gonzales in the Democratic U.S. Senate primary. Reuters framed the result as one of the exceptions to an otherwise strong night for Colorado progressives.

**Amanda Gonzalez wins Democratic secretary of state primary**

Jefferson County Clerk Amanda Gonzalez defeated state Sen. Jessie Danielson in the Democratic primary for Colorado secretary of state. Gonzalez’s win adds to the broader story of progressive and reform-minded candidates gaining traction in statewide and congressional primaries.

**Progressive candidates beat several dark-money-backed statehouse candidates**

Colorado Sun reporting found that dark-money groups spent roughly $2.5 million backing more moderate Democratic statehouse candidates, but those candidates won only two of eight targeted races. The results may shift the balance of power leftward within the Democratic caucus at the state Capitol.

**Colorado wildfire coverage remains important statewide**

Beyond the primary, Colorado continues to face major wildfire and emergency-response stories. AP reported that three firefighters were killed and two injured while responding to a wildfire near the Colorado-Utah border, and Denver7 reported the Gold Mountain Fire in Ouray County had reached more than 18,000 acres with 0% containment as of Thursday Morning.
