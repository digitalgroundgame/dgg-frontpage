Hey everyone, just a quick reminder as we head into the general election.

Primary season has naturally brought about some results that we were thrilled by, and others that have disappointed us, and some that we've been on opposite sides in terms of who we supported. That's all normal because there are genuine ideological differences within a coalition as broad as ours. 

What isn't helpful though is letting those disagreements turn into toxicity toward each other. During the watch party for the Michigan primaries, things got particularly bad, so much so that apparently someone from Ritchie Torres' campaign staff who was checking out our server, left after getting into a snarky and hostile argument with another member. This is a missed opportunity, especially given the fact that Torres is a congressman who is ideologically very much aligned with us. 

Once a primary is over, the focus needs to shift to winning the general election, and that means working with a lot of people who don't agree with us on every issue or every candidate, rather than catastrophizing because your preferred candidate didn't win and lashing out at others who disagree.

If you've seen _The Odyssey_ recently (or read it course lol), think of it as Zeus' Law: treat visitors with hospitality because they just might be a god in disguise. That random member on the server whose mom you're insulting because you're getting into a heated argument, could surprisingly be a campaign staffer, local organizer, journalist, or a smaller content creator that we might want to work with in the future. They all pop into the server from time to time and first impressions matter. These are all potential partners that we don't want to be burning bridges prematurely with.

So debate, disagree, meme, celebrate, complain but just try not to be a dick while doing it. We talk about having a big tent, but it's always easier said than done. Building a broad coalition means leaving the door open for people who aren't 100% like us, and that means genuinely putting an effort to accept differences in views. This is what separates us from the counter productive purity testing that goes on in other organizations, and that's ultimately how we're going to win.

# **Colorado**

## **📰 News**

- ​Voter Registration
  - Coloradans should [check their voter registration](https://www.coloradosos.gov/voter/pages/pub/olvr/findVoterReg.xhtml) through the Colorado Secretary of State’s Go Vote Colorado website. The site allows voters to register, update their voter record, check mail ballot status, view a sample ballot, track or cure a ballot, and find ballot return or in-person voting locations.
- Gubernatorial Debate Disputes Continue
  - Republican gubernatorial candidate Victor Marx has stated that he will only participate in one general-election [debate](https://www.cpr.org/2026/08/10/colorado-governor-candidate-debates/) with Democratic nominee Phil Weiser, and that the debate should be held without a moderator. Marx declined a 9News debate invitation, objecting to a traditional moderated format with timed answers and questions selected by the network.
  - Weiser’s campaign rejected the offer, stating that it would only consider serious debate proposals. Marx has also been criticized for appearing likely to skip the Club 20 debate in Grand Junction, a traditional campaign stop focused on Western Slope issues.
- Democrats Increase CO Congressional Map Targets
  - [National Democrats](https://coloradosun.com/2026/08/05/lauren-boebert-jeff-hurd-targets-national-democrats-dccc/) have expanded their Colorado target map, adding the 3rd and 4th Congressional Districts to their list of districts in play. This comes after the party had already targeted the Republican-held 5th District and the highly competitive 8th District.
  - The move shows Democrats are attempting to compete in all eight Colorado congressional districts, though analysts still rate the 3rd, 4th, and 5th Districts as likely Republican. The strategy appears aimed at forcing Republicans to defend seats that would not normally receive this level of attention.
- Trump DOJ Voter Roll Lawsuit Dismissed
  - A federal court [dismissed](https://coloradonewsline.com/briefs/trump-colorado-voter-data-federal-judge/) the Trump administration’s lawsuit attempting to force Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold to turn over the state’s complete unredacted voter registration database. The Department of Justice had sought voter information including names, dates of birth, addresses, and identifying driver’s license or Social Security information.
  - The dismissal continues a broader conflict between Colorado election officials and the Trump administration over federal access to sensitive voter data. Griswold had refused to provide the complete unredacted file, arguing against handing over private voter information beyond what is publicly available.
