# **Colorado**

## **📰 News**

- ​**Republican gubernatorial result: Victor Marx declared winner**
	- After 9 days of counting, Victor Marx has been declared the [republican nominee](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/07/09/victor-marx-defeats-barb-kirkmeyer-as-colorados-republican-gubernatorial-primary-crosses-finish-line/) for governor. Marx is a Marine veteran, evangelical ministry leader, and first-time candidate from the Colorado Springs area who ran as an outsider against state Sen. Barbara Kirkmeyer and state Rep. Scott Bottoms. The Associated Press called the race for Marx on July 9.
	- The final result was a margin of 2,515 votes, or about 0.48% of the 521,938 votes tallied. Under Colorado law, an automatic recount is triggered when the margin between the top two finishers is 0.5% or less of the leading candidate’s vote total; in this race, Colorado Politics reported that the margin would have needed to shrink to 1,040 votes or fewer to trigger a mandatory recount.
	- The result also leaves Republicans with an immediate unity problem. Marx did not receive endorsements from either of his primary opponents. Colorado Politics reported that Bottoms had characterized Marx as a liar and “con man,” while Kirkmeyer had called Marx unfit for office during the campaign. Kirkmeyer thanked her supporters, and closed her concession with the line: “I still haven’t killed anyone.”
- **Phil Weiser names Lesley Dahlkemper as running mate**
	- Democratic nominee for governor Phil Weiser has announced [Lesley Dahlkemper](https://www.cpr.org/2026/07/22/lesley-dahlkemper-phil-weiser-running-mate/) as his running mate. Dahlkemper serves as the Jefferson county commissioner who was most recently elected for a second term in 2022.
	- Weiser framed the pick less as a campaign-balancing move and more as a governing choice. He told reporters that his first priority was choosing “someone who could be a governing partner,” and specifically someone who could “step in and serve Colorado ably as governor” if needed.
- **GEO employee charged after shooting protester outside Aurora ICE facility**
	- An off-duty GEO Group employee has been charged after allegedly [shooting](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/17/colorado-ice-employee-arrested-shooting-protester) a protester outside the Aurora ICE Processing Center. Reuters reported that Brandon Booth, 42, was charged with first-degree assault and attempted first-degree assault after allegedly firing one shot toward two women protesting outside the GEO-operated ICE facility on July 16. One of the women, Emma Landis, was struck in the foot.
	- According to Reuters, police said two women had a verbal confrontation with GEO employees and were photographing vehicles before Booth got out of his vehicle and fired a single shot. Booth left the scene and was arrested nearby shortly after.
- **Tuberculosis case confirmed at Adams County ICE detention facility**
	- Adams County health officials confirmed a detainee with [active tuberculosis](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/07/21/geo-impeding-tb-investigation-at-aurora-ice-detention-center-colorado-officials-say) at the GEO-operated facility last month, and the county later issued a public health order directing GEO to provide access to medical records, identify potential exposures, and allow investigators to interview the patient.
	- Officials said GEO had not provided the information or access needed for Adams County Health Department and Denver Health’s tuberculosis clinic to conduct a public health investigation. Without that cooperation, health officials said they could not determine the scope of potential exposure.
- **Competing income tax proposals could both be on the November ballot**
	- A coalition called Protect Colorado’s Future is gathering signatures for a graduated income tax proposal that would lower income taxes for Coloradans making under $500,000 while raising taxes on those making more than that.
	- Advance Colorado, the conservative group backing a competing income-tax-cap measure, said it turned in nearly 190,000 signatures. Initiative No. 232 would cap Colorado’s individual and corporate income tax rate at 4.4%. That rate has been in place since 2022.
