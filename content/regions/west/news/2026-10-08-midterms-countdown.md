## **📰 News**

- **Older News from 9/24**
  - Ballots are being sent out for the election
    - Colorado's general election is entering the home stretch. Every statewide office is on the ballot, and voters will also decide [14 statewide ballot questions](https://ballotpedia.org/Colorado_2026_ballot_measures).
    - Those measures cover everything from income taxes and fentanyl penalties to immigration enforcement, mail-ballot identification, redistricting, natural gas, transgender youth policies and Front Range passenger rail. Constitutional amendments require 55% support to pass.rejected.
    - Amendment 81 would specifically require state and local law enforcement to notify the Department of Homeland Security in certain cases involving people charged with violent crimes or people with prior felony convictions when their lawful presence cannot be determined. It would override existing Colorado restrictions on local cooperation with federal immigration authorities.
  - Mail Voting Is Becoming Its Own Election Fight
    - Colorado voters will decide [Initiative 362](https://leg.colorado.gov/initiatives/mail-ballot-voter-identification-362), which would require voters returning mail ballots to provide a driver's-license number, REAL ID number or the last four digits of their Social Security number. Colorado currently authenticates most mail ballots through signature verification. Supporters argue the additional identification would strengthen verification; opponents argue it could cause otherwise eligible ballots to be rejected.
    - The state is simultaneously dealing with federal changes to voter verification. On Friday, the U.S. Supreme Court allowed the Trump administration to move forward with a federal voter-verification database combining Social Security information with citizenship records while litigation continues.
  - Weiser and Marx Head Toward October
    - Democrat Phil Weiser and Republican Victor Marx are competing to replace term-limited Gov. Jared Polis, with Colorado's continuing budget problems likely to be waiting for whoever takes office. Lawmakers dealt with roughly a $1.5 billion shortfall this year, and the structural budget problem is expected to continue into the next administration.
    - The candidates still do not have a traditional gubernatorial debate scheduled after their planned October appearance on the Aaron Harber Show fell apart over the format and moderator. They are currently scheduled to appear together at an [October 8 Colorado Chamber forum](https://www.9news.com/article/news/politics/weiser-marx-joint-forum-colorado-chamber-removes-9news-moderator/73-8801686d-d736-4b4a-9b76-988c6359c304), but that event is not being presented as a debate.
