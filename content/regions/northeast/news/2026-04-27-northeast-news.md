## Maine

On Friday Governor Mills vetoed the bill that would have established a first in the nation moratorium on data center construction preventing any construction until November 2027. Mills gave her [reasoning](https://www.maine.gov/) for the veto in a letter to the legislature saying "A moratorium is appropriate given the impacts of massive data centers in other states on the environment and on electricity rates.

But the final version of this bill fails to allow for a [specific project in the Town of Jay](https://themainemonitor.org/) that enjoys strong local support from its host community and region." The town of Jay is home to the former Androscoggin Mill, the site of the proposed data center; the mill closed down in 2023 after struggling for years following a fire in 2020.

Proponents of the data center claim it could bring up to 800-1000 construction jobs as well as 125 permanent jobs to the area all while allegedly having its own power source requiring no improvement to the grid and no increases on local energy rates.

## New Hampshire

An overview of the candidates running in the democratic senate primary. This week we will cover Chris Pappas, the current front runner.

Chris Pappas, has served in the NH executive council (think of it as an advisor to the governor from the districts [4th district]) from 2013-2018. He's also served in the US house representing the 1st district, since 2019. Pappas is highly bipartisan, (like his predecessor Shaheen).

He [voted against legalizing weed](https://web.archive.org/), [criticized Biden over student debt relief](https://www.wmur.com/), [voted for the Laken Riley act](https://www.wmur.com/), and [voted to censure Rashida Tlaib](https://pappas.house.gov/) (over saying "from the river to the sea").

However, he is a reliable Democrat, he [voted for the CHIPS and Science Act](https://pappas.house.gov/), [the Infrastructure and Jobs Act](https://pappas.house.gov/), [Inflation Reduction Act](https://pappas.house.gov/), and [the American Rescue Plan](https://pappas.house.gov/).

## Vermont

Melo Grant being removed as chair of Burlington's Public Safety Committee feels like more than just a disagreement on the city council. It reflects a broader conversation happening in Vermont politics about how progressive leadership responds to concerns around public safety, economic stability, and effective government.

While the conflict shows real tension between Progressives and Democrats over policy and leadership, it may also suggest some priorities are moving closer to a more moderate Democratic approach focused on practicality, cooperation, and results. In a very heavily progressive state like Vermont, that does not have to mean a rejection of those values, but rather a recognition that they have to be paired with policies that can earn wider public support.

The controversy could represent an opportunity rather than a point of division. It may be a reminder that for Progressives in Vermont, lasting reform often depends on working more cooperatively with Democrats and building policies that appeal beyond activist circles.

If that leads to stronger alignment between Vermont's progressive and moderate wings, it could help create a more unified and effective Democratic coalition, one better able to respond to voters, advance practical reforms, and strengthen the party statewide.

## Connecticut

This week, the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP) released its triennial Climate Progress Report, outlining the state's significant progress toward statutory goals for reducing statewide greenhouse gas emissions. The DEEP report forecasts a 34% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions from 2001 levels by 2030, underscoring years of state investment in clean energy and pollution control.

Connecticut has formed sustainable energy partnerships with the Millstone and Seabrook nuclear power plants, which have lowered electricity prices for residents through long-term fixed-price agreements. Furthermore, the DEEP report stressed the crucial role of interstate cooperation in reducing GHG emissions, especially in the absence of federal direction. Following the Trump administration's withdrawal from the Paris Climate Agreement in 2017, a group of U.S. states established the U.S.

Climate Alliance, a coordinated state initiative to uphold the principles of the Paris Agreement. Since its inception, the 24-state coalition has collectively reduced GHG emissions by 24% and increased the group's GDP by 34%, showcasing the effectiveness of interstate climate action and the economic benefits of green energy policy.

## Washington D.C.

With less than two months to go for the start of the DC mayoral primary, the candidates had their first mayoral debate. Six candidates presented their plans to tackle rising housing and utility costs. The two front runners (Janeese Lewis George and Kenyan McDuffie), sparred over their records of delivering on this issue for Washingtonians. Ballots start being delivered to primary voters on June 16th in the District's first ranked-choice voting primary.
