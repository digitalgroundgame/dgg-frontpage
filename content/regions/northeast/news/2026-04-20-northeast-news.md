## Connecticut

Citing the January killings of American citizens by federal officers in Minneapolis, Connecticut Senate majority leader Bob Duff (D-Norwalk) led the charge on April 14th for the passage of a bill that would seek to safeguard Connecticut from the misconduct of federal officers and lack of federal accountability.

Senate Bill 397, An Act Concerning Democracy and Government Accountability, includes provisions that require all law enforcement officers to display visible identification (badge and name tag) on their person and generally prohibit law enforcement officers from wearing facial coverings while conducting their duties.

Additionally, it designates locations such as schools, hospitals, state and municipal buildings, and places of worship as "protected areas" where law enforcement officers are prohibited from detaining or arresting any individual suspected of a civil offense without a judicial warrant. Broadly, the bill would further protect the civil liberties of Connecticut residents and expand their ability to take civil action against federal law enforcement officers.

Now that SB 397 has passed in the Senate, it is slated for debate and voting in the Connecticut House of Representatives.

## New York

The Trump admin to resume funding on the Second Avenue after a 6 month standoff with the MTA. The funding freeze began due to the Trump DOT expressing DEI concerns with the MTA policies. The MTA states they have complied with the DOT rules since they were out in place, and should not have taken 6 months and a lawsuit to resolve. The USDOT will no have to pay an additional 60 million in reimbursements along with the initial funding.

## Pennsylvania

On April 15th a man started gagging and lost consciousness inside an ICE detention facility in Clearfield County. Staff failed to give him proper medical attention and told other detainees to help him. His body was white, he was shaking and sweating, and he threw up a green substance according to witnesses. This is the third person to be hospitalized facing similar symptoms according to an unnamed witness.

As a result of this incident 70 people from his block began a hunger strike, which escalated to about 100 people by the 17th. The detainees are pushing for better medical care from GEO Group, which operates Moshannon Valley Processing Center, and changes to the food given to detainees. Lenny Oddo, facility administrator of Moshannon Valley Processing Center, declined to comment about the hunger strike, citing concerns for the privacy of detainees.

## Delaware

Earlier this week, Delaware lawmakers made great progress in limiting the scope of ICE's powers in the state. One bill, HS 2 for HB 94, was approved by the House on Thursday the 16th, and three others were presented before the House Judiciary Committee. HS 2 for HB 94 targets state and local law enforcement and prevents them from assisting federal agents in "child-serving entities, institutions of higher education, places of worship, or health-care facilities".

This doesn't stop ICE themselves from pursuing individuals in these areas as they are a federal agency, however it aims to make them think twice as they're significantly less likely to receive help from the police. This bill is moving on to seek Senate approval. In addition, HB 366, 367, and 368 were all presented before the House.

The first two target the concerns many have in identifying federal agents and verifying their legitimacy, preventing masks/face coverings and requiring identification to be displayed for LEOs. The final bill aims to set limits around the detainment of those targeted by ICE with exceptions for violent felons and sex offenders. One of the stated goals of this legislation is to help harbor more trusting relations between law enforcement and the community.

The three of these bills are slated to be voted on next week.

Article from [WDEL](https://www.wdel.com/)

## Washington D.C.

The two front-runners in the DC mayoral race have emerged: Former D.C. Councilmember Kenyan McDuffie and current Councilmember representing Ward 4 Janeese Lewis George. Both candidates are prioritizing the same three major planks of their proposed policy agenda: Housing Affordability, Utility Affordability, and asserting DC Home Rule over the federal government and fighting back against the Trump administration.

Given the deep blue electorate of DC (there has never been a republican mayor of the city), this primary is essentially the election for Mayor of the City.
