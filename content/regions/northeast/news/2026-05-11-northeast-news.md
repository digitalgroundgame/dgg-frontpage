## Maine

Rep. Chellie Pingree of Maine's first district traveled to Texas on Wednesday for an official oversight visit and to advocate for the release of Olivia Andre an asylum seeker that had been held at the Dilley Immigration Processing center since November of 2025. Andre along with her mother, brother and sister are all asylum seekers from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, they were all taken into ICE custody together but her mother and siblings were released in March.

While touring the facility Pingree was able to visit Olivia and hours later a federal judge ordered that she be released no later than Friday she was reunited with her family and friends later that night.

Articles from [WMTW](https://www.wmtw.com/) and [News Center Maine](https://www.newscentermaine.com/)

## New Hampshire

Last week a major healthcare package was passed by the legislature and will likely be signed by the governor. The first [bill](https://legiscan.com/) requires insurance and Medicare to cover biomarker testing, sponsored by republicans. The second [bill](https://legiscan.com/) makes available short term non-ACA complaint insurance plans, sponsored by republicans. The final [bill](https://legiscan.com/) requires Medicare to cover GLP-1s, sponsored by democrats.

## Vermont

"SERIOUSLY!" Vermont's lone U.S. House representative, Becca Balint, a Democrat, has officially announced her reelection campaign and filed paperwork to seek a third two-year term in Congress. Balint recently gained national attention after a tense exchange with Trump-appointed Attorney General Pam Bondi during a House Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on the Epstein files.

In announcing her campaign, Balint said her top priority is defending Vermonters from federal overreach and "attacks against our fundamental freedoms," specifically citing reproductive rights and voting rights.

Balint is not expected to face a serious Democratic primary challenge. Republican Mark Coester, whom she defeated in 2024, is running again, but Balint enters the race with a significant fundraising advantage. Her campaign priorities include restoring enhanced Affordable Care Act subsidies, investing in affordable housing, and redirecting federal spending away from policies she opposes, including expanded immigration enforcement and the war with Iran.

Article from [VT Digger](https://vtdigger.org/)

## New York

The Preliminary vote passed on the rent stabilized range, setting that range to between 0-2% on 1 year leases and 0-4% on two year leases. Of the nine member board, seven voted yes, one voted no, one abstained. The six Mamdani board appointments voted five yes, one abstained. It appeared most in attendance were happy with the range. Renters are unhappy that an increase is still on the table, but have some hope that the final vote will have a rent freeze on the table.

Landlords say the max of 2% a year is not enough to keep up with expenses. For perspective, the [2025 rent stabilized rates](https://rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us/) were 3% on 1 year leases, and 4.5% on 2 year leases. The final vote will be held on June 25th.

Article from [ABC 7 NY](https://abc7ny.com/)

## Delaware

On Wednesday, May 6, the Delaware state Senate passed HB 151, a bill focused on putting restrictions on private detention facilities that are often used by ICE. It seems to not be an immediate problem, but this bill is being put in place in an effort to get ahead of the issue. This bill will be advanced to Governor Matt Meyer.

Article from [Delaware Republic](https://www.delawarepublic.org/)

In addition, HB 216 was introduced to the house the week prior on April 30. This bill is aimed at dispelling concerns of dark money influences political campaigns in Delaware. Some key points are requiring PACs that contribute more than $2000 to register with the state, and for registered PACs to list individuals or entities that are affiliated with or control the committee

## Virginia

The Virginia State Supreme Court struck down the redistricting referendum. This leaves in place the previously drawn maps that currently sit at 6D-5R. The Yes vote to draw new maps favoring Democrats 10-1 narrowly passed at 51.7% to 48.3% on April 21st, but is now null and void. The previously drawn maps are generally considered to be "fair" and Democrats are expected to pick up at least one, potentially two, seats in the 2026 midterms, even without the redistricting effort.

Article from [Axios](https://www.axios.com/)
