## 🏙️ Lay of the La La Land: The 2026 LA Mayoral Race

Hey DGG,

The 2026 race for Mayor of Los Angeles is looking up to turn into a massive rumble in the concrete jungle for the soul of a city that is going to be at a crossroads come this November just like the rest of the country in the midterms that fall on the same night. Incumbent Mayor Karen Bass is up for reelection and it looked like she was going to cruise to reelection at first even though lots of people throughout the city criticized her handling of the insane wildfires last January. Developer Rick Caruso initially decided to run again and that would've been a rematch of their 2022 race which Bass won, but he decided to not run again for mayor or for statewide office (good riddance).

Now she's facing a challenge from Nithya Raman, City Councilmember out of District 4 who jumped in the race fairly late. Raman is a much younger candidate than Bass at 44 years old, something voters have been clamoring for at the ballot box like what happened in NYC with Mamdani becoming the youngest mayor to get elected in the city's entire history, and she might have the most potential to unseat Bass. I've made a detailed breakdown of the major liberal candidates below that goes over their backgrounds, and their plans to fix a city that's been dealing with unfettered rates of homelessness, housing affordability, and public safety for over two decades.

P.S: Spencer Pratt is also running (as a Republican), but I don't think I need to explain why he's not a good choice, this rundown is centered on the Democratic candidates in the race.

### Backgrounds of each candidate and how they plan to address certain issues facing

**The Incumbent: Karen Bass**

- **Background:** Before she was the 43rd Mayor of Los Angeles in 2022, Bass built a career as a consensus-builder and legislator. She served in the California State Assembly and made history as the first African American woman to serve as Speaker. After that stint, she spent over a decade in Congress in the U.S. House of Representatives. She also has a heavy background in community organizing and social work, founding the Community Coalition in South LA back in the 1990s to address issues like poverty and substance abuse.
- **Stance on Key Issues:**
  - **Homelessness:** This was the central battleground of her first term. Bass's signature policy has been "Inside Safe," a program that moved people from homeless encampments into motels, hotels, or interim housing. She argued that this "housing-first" approach was a humane and effective way to get people off the streets and get them connected to services. The counter arguments to her claim were that her policy was too slow and expensive for how ineffective it was, and that it doesn't stop the influx of rapid people who are becoming homeless. The number of homelessness is slowly growing across the entire country with housing costs and basic necessities becoming luxuries because of the sky rocketing of prices in just the past year.
  - **Housing:** Bass supports streamlining affordable housing development and pushed to use city-owned land for new projects. Her approach in her first term was more focused on preserving existing affordable housing stock and building subsidized units instead of the sweeping upzoning advocated by the YIMBY (Yes In My Backyard) movement.
  - **Public Safety:** Bass has walked a moderate line with public safety. She opposed defunding the police and maintained the budget for the LAPD. However, she also invested in alternative response programs, dispatching mental health workers (instead of armed police) to non-violent crisis calls. She emphasized police reform and rebuilding trust between the community and the LAPD.

**The New Challenger: Nithya Raman**

- **Background:** Raman is a city planner and urbanist who first won her city council seat in 2020 by defeating a longtime incumbent, becoming the first candidate to unseat an incumbent councilmember in decades, so there's no reason she can't unseat Bass if she does well in the primary behind Bass. Before winning her election in 2020, she worked with the nonprofit Time's Up (addressing sexual harassment in the workplace) and founded the Selva Foundation, which worked on rural development and housing rights in India. She lives in Silver Lake with her family and has a PhD from MIT. Her brand is technocratic progressivism; she is equally comfortable discussing zoning codes and mutual aid.
- **Stance on Key Issues:**
  - **Homelessness:** Raman is the architect of the "inside-out" approach. While supportive of Bass's Inside Safe, she emphasizes the need for prevention (keeping people in their homes) and rapidly building permanent supportive housing. She has been a vocal critic of simply moving encampments from one block to another without offering long-term solutions. She advocates for converting hotels and motels into permanent housing and streamlining the approval of tiny home villages.
  - **Housing:** Raman is the strongest YIMBY (Yes In My Backyard) in the race. She supports upzoning single-family neighborhoods near transit, eliminating minimum parking requirements (to lower construction costs), and ending "councilmanic prerogative" (the informal rule allowing councilmembers to veto projects in their district). She views the housing shortage as the root cause of homelessness and unaffordability.
  - **Public Safety:** She supports a re-imagined public safety framework. While she votes for LAPD funding, she has been a leading voice for expanding the unarmed crisis response teams and diverting mental health calls away from armed police. She emphasizes accountability and has pushed for body camera transparency.

**Rae Huang (Democratic Socialist)**

- **Background:** Huang is a Dem socialist organizer and labor activist. (Note: Depending on her previous electoral history, I think she's a first-time candidate or have run for lower office previously). Her background is in tenant organizing and fighting against corporate landlords. She represents the Bernie Sanders/Warren wing of the left, focusing on class struggle and systemic change.
- **Stance on Key Issues:**
  - **Homelessness:** Huang argues that homelessness is a failure of capitalism, not just governance. Her platform likely centers on a "right to housing" through a massive public works program to build social housing—publicly owned, permanently affordable units. She would likely push for a vacancy tax on empty units and aggressive rent control measures.
  - **Housing:** She's skeptical of market-rate development, even if upzoned. Huang opposes most forms of luxury development and has made the argument that only 100% affordable, publicly financed housing solves the crisis without gentrifying neighborhoods.
  - **Public Safety:** Huang is the most left on this issue. She likely supports a significant reallocation of funds from the LAPD to community-based violence intervention programs and social services. She views over-policing as a racial justice issue.

**Personal Opinion:**

Bass is running on her record. She's going to point to "Inside Safe" as a proof of concept and argue that she has brought stability back to City Hall after the chaos of the 2022 leaked audio scandal involving Councilwoman Martinez. She is the institutionalist choice, backed by the traditional Democratic power structure.

**Why I think Nithya Raman is the Best Pick for Mayor (For the DGG Liberal)**

If you are a liberal who resides in Los Angeles, here's the case on why you should vote for Raman in the primary:

1. **The "Housing First" Purist:** Bass's "Inside Safe" moves people indoors, but it doesn't build enough new units to stop the tide. Raman understands that Los Angeles has a supply problem. She is the only candidate with the technical expertise to actually fix the zoning code and bulldoze the bureaucratic hurdles that prevent building. For a liberal who wants a functional city, Raman offers the most competent handle on the city's #1 issue.

2. **Electable and Effective (The Huang Distinction):** This section is the important part for DGG users in Los Angeles. Rae Huang belongs to the leftist, anti-capitalist wing. I think her heart is in the right place, but her policies (defund-adjacent rhetoric, blanket opposition to market-rate housing) are an electoral liability in a general election against a more moderate or a business-backed candidate, she doesn't have the backing or charisma that Mamdani had in NYC and Mamdani also had the advantage of being an assembly member for a few years by the time he ran. Raman, by contrast, is a governance-focused liberal progressive. She's worked with developers when she's had to, she negotiated with labor, and she passed legislation on the City Council. She has a four-year track record on the Council of actually moving the needle, so her record is proof that liberals can still win in LA without scaring the suburbs.

3. **The Fresh Face vs. The Status Quo:** Bass is a beloved figure locally, but she represents the old guard Democratic Party and a lot of voters on both sides of the political spectrum have already called for there to be less older politicians in office. Raman represents the new coalition of young voters, urbanists, and renters who are frustrated that the city moves too slowly. She can make the case that Bass has had one term to turn the ship, and while she's steadied it, she hasn't changed its course (I.e her mishandling of the Palisades fire).

4. **Coherent Vision on Public Safety:** Raman offers the most coherent third way on safety. She isn't "defund," but she isn't "more cops equals safer streets." She argues that a safe city is one with lights on the streets, mental health responders available, and trust between communities and the LAPD. For liberal voters who feel tired of the false binary between "law and order" and "abolition," Raman gives the city a path forward that's more nuanced and data-driven.

**The Verdict:**

I think Raman is the one candidate who combines the energy of the younger wing of the Democratic Party along with the governing competence of the center. She can beat Bass by arguing for a bolder, faster approach to the same goals that she voted for on LA City Council during Bass' first term, and she can out-organize Rae Huang by showing her legislative wins that are legit. Huang, for all the hype she's gotten from Hasan, hasn't gotten close to the amount of donations and support that Mamdani got. For a liberal looking to actually fix Los Angeles, I think Raman's the most exciting and viable option in the race at the moment especially with Austin Beutner dropping out and she has a solid chance to beat Bass if she comes in one of the two slots in June's primary election.
