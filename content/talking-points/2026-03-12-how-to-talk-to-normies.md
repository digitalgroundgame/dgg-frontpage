General Guide for Persuasion, Rhetoric, and Debate Applicable to All Topics

## Introduction

For those who find themselves in a political organization which spawned from an online influencer’s community, engaging with normal, regular, boring people can be a demanding task. The online politics space is a privileged arena as the creators of memes, video edits, Tik Tok commentary, or Substack articles about a political topic tend to have a disproportionate amount of sway over public opinion. Since 2016, the role of online actors has shifted from a disheveled group of trolls and hobbyists to a veteran, sometimes foreign-funded, professional class which is still driving discourse.(3)

Where memes and politics-as-entertainment content falls flat is that it is not usually designed to change minds. Rather, it emboldens the base of a given ideology, strengthening their prior beliefs and encouraging them to spread messaging far and wide. The goal is to create the repetition of talking points and entertaining stories inside the mind of a normal person. This content crawls within the ears of innocent people and festers, poisoning the victim’s echoic memory until the only detail they can recall after reading the name “Joseph R. Biden” is a clip of the former president struggling on a staircase.

To convince people in a more direct way therefore requires the knowledge that the framing of so much recent history is lost. Only then can it be disentangled and replaced with a more accurate ideology. This article is a guide on how to fight back in such unfriendly territory. Of course, if there was a silver bullet tutorial on changing minds, this nation would not look as it does now. Many competent and academically-minded people have been analyzing rhetoric, persuasion, and debate for lifetimes and it is by no means a solved game. That is no excuse to give up now. The remaining sections of this introduction will guide the reader on the source material used and some interesting historical precedents to the current timeline.

## On Tyranny

A common saying that one might hear from normies goes something like “those who do not learn from history will surely repeat it!” Anyone with a background in history will explain why this quote haunts their mind at every second of the day. What could be wrong with well-meaning people wanting to study so that they can improve the present? In a vacuum, nothing. But in practice, the average person is not aware of the copious present-day cultural symbols and beliefs which bias any study of the past. It is easy to look back and see that the genocidal regime which ruled Nazi Germany was evil. It is even easier to find a grab-bag of aesthetically sort-of-evil-looking traits which exist both in the 20th and 21st centuries, and then delude oneself into thinking that history has been solved. “I’ve figured it out! It is bad when the government makes racial considerations and wants to tax citizens and confiscate guns!” Congratulations, this formula easily creates a typical libertarian-leaning reactionary Conservative. They are now convinced that Hitler was on the Left and that the Democratic Party are the bad guys of 2020s. After all, they were defending slavery only a hundred years ago, it’s just common sense.

The work of historian Timothy Snyder is painfully relevant in the 21st century. While people in the present day might read about the Holocaust and only see a series of unjustifiable and inhuman actions, it remains the role of historians to explain what caused such events. In his book On Tyranny, published shortly after the 2016 election, Snyder constructs a set of prescriptions which the reader must follow to avoid tyranny. Interestingly, his advice for the reader adheres to personal or societal efforts which may not appear to have a direct link to political change. To anyone paying attention, it is clear that the gradual degradation of the truth in the media environment has been caustic. The effects of loneliness and how it incubates radical actors and audiences are on full display. The trust shared in daily life towards strangers, friends, and family alike has gone missing. True change does seem to begin at the level of the individual, a fact which means that small acts of resistance can make all the difference.

Timothy Snyder’s perspective will color this article but his work cannot easily bridge the gap between digital and grassroots political change. Members of a radical Liberal online movement have the unique opportunity to alter both the online media space and real-life. As such, this article will offer advice for reaching out to normies in the context of keyboard warrioring or hardcore bloodsports debate, and also in a canvassing operation or phonebanking. Some strategies however will bleed between both contexts.(10)

## Disclaimer: Talking to MAGA

Author: @marlow_kurtz

While it is true that left-leaning people ought to work harder to find points of agreement and coalesce, I think that having boldfaced disagreements with MAGA voters can only result in the confirmation of their bias that we are all evil.

When it comes to discussions with MAGA voters, this disclaimer leans on the advice of Dr. Lee McIntyre, American researcher and professor of ethics and philosophy. In his political pamphlet “On Disinformation,” McIntyre makes the argument that MAGA politicians are engaging in a deliberate, coordinated campaign against the truth, itself. These malefactors are conducting the exact same kind of denialist campaign that big tobacco invented in the 1950s, following the exact same formula. I will reference Dr. McIntyre’s work as I offer my analysis on how we should dialogue with our MAGA neighbors in our small community.

The most important thing to remember is that MAGA voters are victims of the messaging of their political party. They have had their biases, fears, bigotry, and beliefs twisted into unimaginably uncomfortable shapes to inspire the kind of coalescing fear that the MAGA politicians need in order to whip their base into line. The worst of their hatred for others has been manufactured, and you can hear them regurgitating slogans and easy-to-remember talking points rather than their own personal opinions on matters of bigotry. Our president, himself, is the source of most of the genuine fear that MAGA voters feel for the country, and they have been reduced down to their most basic impulses by that ever-increasing weight of stress.

The next key fact to keep in mind when dealing with a MAGA voter is that they largely cannot be reasoned into believing the truth. The MAGA movement represents a pendulum swing in response to decades of leftist identity politics; the right got tired of left wing high horses and grandstanding. Now the right has learned to redefine reality much better than any “radical left lunatic” ever could. McIntyre argues that “denialist beliefs are not based on facts” but that they are instead “rooted in identity.” When we, with our "woke, DEI" identities present ourselves to the MAGA voters in our community, their system of biases assume us to be incorrect based on the fact of who we are rather than on the evidence we can cite for our beliefs. McIntyre specifies that the disinformation campaign MAGA conducts on its voters isn’t just about getting them to agree with whatever Donald Trump says,”but to erode [their] trust in the ‘truth tellers’ on the other side.” The MAGA media machine has been incredibly effective in accomplishing this goal, as all sources of information outside of MAGA are viewed with extreme skepticism by MAGA voters. As McIntyre points out, “mere doubt can be overcome with evidence, but distrust cannot.”(7)

If liberals are capable of exercising sympathy for the homeless, for refugees, or for addicts, then we have all we need to exercise sympathy for our deluded compatriots. We are the only adults in the room now, and that means being patient with MAGA voters. It means understanding what in their identity and personal feelings led them to be susceptible to this kind of propaganda. It means years of rebuilding their trust in us as their fellow countrymen. These are people who need to be healed from decades of cultish faith, not people who have simply missed the right answers.

Be a good neighbor. Be a patient listener.

Our true fight is with those who lied to our brothers.

## Believe in Something

Author: Evan Simonetti

Snyder explains in lesson 10 of On Tyranny that a descent into nihilism and disregard for truth will create the conditions for tyranny: “If nothing is true, then no one can criticize power, because there is no basis upon which to do so. If nothing is true, then all is spectacle. The biggest wallet pays for the most blinding lights.”(10) The antidote to a post-truth or anti-truth environment is an individual who believes in something. Social media has poisoned discourse such that genuinity often comes across as cringe or soy. Recently, however, memes have steered in a more positive direction, rewarding individuals for aura farming or singling out examples of rage bait so that they can be ignored.

If the liberal movement wants to embrace truth, it may be necessary to research how the right has become so successful peddling outright lies. The obsession with finding a “Joe Rogan of the left” is therefore a worthy crisis. Mr. Rogan has been a superweapon capable of mass-radicalizing normies into adopting the aesthetics and beliefs of the online reactionary conservative coalition. There are aspects of Mr. Rogan’s personality and platform that Liberals ought to emulate. The following section will therefore task the reader with identifying the strategies of Mr. Rogan’s media machine.

## The Joe Rogan Khanate

Many have mocked him. Many have called him a liar, a fraud, and an evil person. Deep down, however, all know the truth. Mr. Rogan is actually just an unfunny human being.(11) The Joe Rogan “Experience” is characterized not by the eponymous host, but rather its guests. A viral meme describes the atmosphere of Mr. Rogan’s podcast perfectly (Figure 1).

Figure 1. Posted by Anonymous, September 27, 2020. Meme from KnowYourMeme, Posted May 27, 2021. https://knowyourmeme.com/photos/2110266-joe-rogan.(1)

Whoever constructed this meme was closer to the truth than they may have realized. The portrayal of Mongol culture in Medieval travel writing can present insights for the phenomenon of Mr. Rogan and much more. One of the most famous works of travel writing in all of history is The Travels of Marco Polo, in which the titular Venetian merchant journeys across the known world. He spent around two decades in China, at that time ruled by Kublai Khan, grandson of Genghis Khan. What fascinated the mind of Marco Polo and readers of his work for centuries were his descriptions of the Mongol court. The genre of travel writing lends itself to sharing information about exotic ideas, objects, peoples, and events. In addition, the audience of Polo’s work in the Middle Ages would have had this cultural context:

​"The ruler constitutes a cosmic center, an “unmoved mover,” whose majesty pulls the world’s diversity into his or her orbit. The distance traveled by an exotic object to that center is an index of the ruler’s wealth and power. The greater the number of distant places represented by such objects, the better."

This audience could then analogize the Mongol court, full of exotic objects, with their own rulers in the west. In a twenty-first century context, most western nations have abandoned monarchy, instead placing the role of governance within a nation’s citizens.

## Distant Individuals

​This shift of power to the individual removed the distance between elites and peasants, enabling anybody to imagine themselves as the center of the nation. In other words, Mr. Rogan’s podcast environment is a contemporary equivalent to the court of a Mongol Khan. He brings together representatives from various regions of the world, layers of society, and levels of education. What Legassie calls the prestige economy of long-distance knowledge is in effect, something he describes as “a constellation of symbolic conventions, material practices, and structures of feeling that enabled medieval people to accrue social, political, and economic advantage through their association with the foreign.”(6) This phenomenon functioned in the medieval world because travel through vast distances was inherently dangerous, costly, and difficult. Because of these factors, the knowledge gained about distant lands was highly valued and trusted. In the present day, there are equivalent barriers to accessing high-profile individuals. Even if these barriers are self-imposed.

​Figures like RFK Jr. and Elon Musk offer Mr. Rogan’s audience key insights into worlds that they feel disconnected from. RFK Jr., an antivaxxer and conspiracy theorist, pretends to have experience in the pharmaceutical and medical industries. Elon Musk, an “entrepreneur” who has worked with the US government as a contractor because he really likes science fiction video games and movies, can pretend to have meaningful things to say about how the government can be more efficient. Since the members of Mr. Rogan’s audience are ideologically calcified, they will never research any claims that guests on the podcast make. The credibility of these claims instead arises from the exotic nature of the guests. It is not a coincidence that these two people have unique speaking patterns. Musk is an immigrant from South Africa and also self-identifies as having Autism Spectrum Disorder, whereas RFK Jr.’s voice quivers because of a brainworm Spasmodic Dysphonia.(4, 5) In summary, the aesthetics of these guests matches their exotic backgrounds and therefore bolsters the claims they make.

# Performing as a Liberal

## Validate

​It is important to remember that the vast majority of Americans who contributed to the wrong political party winning the 2025 election were misled by bad faith actors, discouraged through algorithmic depression, and lost faith in American economic prosperity. The most concerning categories are Liberals who were dissuaded from participating or center-leaning individuals that were convinced to vote for the wrong party. These patriots are the primary targets for canvassing and phonebanking operations. There is no justification to insult these people or pretend that their problems are imaginary, as is custom for the hardcore political arena. Instead, find ways to relate to the issues and beliefs that they prioritize. Once they feel validated, it is easier for them to trust new information from an outside source. After spreading Liberal messaging, return to the validation by explaining how specific politicians are dedicated to solving this individual’s problems. Phonebanking training materials refer to this tactic as “the validation sandwich.”

## Be Welcoming

​Political organizations (including Digital Ground Game) encourage participation by cultivating a welcoming atmosphere within the community. This might sound like basic socialization but its importance should not be understated. When approaching people that you want to persuade, showing them that they have a legitimate place within your movement can make or break an encounter. Discussing politics is a social faux-pas at best when meeting new people, so if there are ways to break the ice by talking about video games, movies, the weather, or anything else you genuinely enjoy, this can ease tensions. If a conversation ends with the other party believing that they decided to change their mind, go out to vote, or research a specific issue, this is a great success. A welcoming environment promotes agency and passion.

## Long Distance Knowledge

As a canvasser or phonebanker, you will often be speaking to people who live in distant areas of the United States, far from your hometown. While canvassing for the 2020 Georgia Senate runoff election, Destiny encountered a hostile MAGA voter who accused him of being a paid activist because he was traveling quite far for a simple doorknocking operation. DGG Canvassers need to be prepared for situations like this, but it may be possible to spin outside influences as a positive. Embrace the fact that you are volunteering for political action outside of your state because you see similar issues happening in both areas. Establish yourself as a representative of the region you hail from and give a brief explanation for how Republicans have damaged your region’s economy or government. It might even help to be as stereotypical as possible to really sell yourself to the other person. Distance might appear in ways other than geography however. If you have specialized knowledge from a college degree, tie this into your rhetoric. If you know someone personally within the government or an industry affected by MAGA politics, mention their stories. The prestige economy of long distance knowledge works like an advanced form of a personal anecdote in a debate. It is nearly impossible to disregard this anecdote without feeling like an asshole or ultimate skeptic. Additionally, people want to learn more about the unknown. It would be unsatisfying to reject new knowledge about a region or area of expertise because of a political difference. Remember also that an in-person interaction is more memorable than all of the random noise from the internet and media.

# Debating as a Liberal

## Invalidate

​If Liberals truly believe that the Illiberal elements of our political environment have limited redeeming value, it is necessary to act like this during hostile encounters. Imagine for a second how effective gaslighting can be as a tactic. Now imagine how far a political movement can go if it gaslit while telling truths. Since each statement of fact is mindmelting to the average hardcore MAGA voter, Liberals must weaponize this psychological response. Do not hesitate to reshape their understanding of reality. They will attempt to gain concessions where the conservative narrative feels at least somewhat correct, (Biden has dementia, Russiagate/J6 were overhyped, immigrants commit crimes sometimes) but this is why it is essential to not give ground. If an illiberal person knows that they can be comforted on command, they will never have to reckon with the false reality they inhabit. Liberals consume statements from MAGA thought leaders and instantly see the signals of lies, hidden truths, corruption, logical fallacies, and appeals to power over truth. These falsehoods become easier to identify for those with self-doubt, and this is exactly what “truth gaslighting” trains.

## Targeting Audiences In-Person

​This could refer to an in-person debate (such as Unf*ck America or Pangburn events) or a hardcore debate with friends, family, or even random people at a protest. As a debater at an event, a Liberal should take maximum advantage of their possession of a microphone and note how much time they have for each debate segment. Conservatives take pleasure in clip-farming unprepared and uneducated debaters. For question and answer segments, conservatives respond indignantly to questions from the crowd (while not necessarily targeting the question-asker) to create no wiggle room for disagreement. Possessing a small amount of shamelessness to emulate this may be helpful. Make an effort to read the crowd as well. If they react to jokes or insults then continue feeding them. For less formal debates, remaining unmoveable is a priority. Present your Liberal beliefs as simply as possible and affirm this by calling them common sense (or create your own Liberal version of this term to disassociate it with MAGA). Reference the least articulate and most extreme members of the opposing political movement and act as though they represent it as a whole, forcing either a concession or an uninspired defense of the indefensible. Tell them about the “reasonable” opposition who concede heavily on important issues and make it clear that these are the only ones you can respect.

## Targeting Online Audiences

​The internet is an amazing place but it is always difficult to give advice about anything “online” because the environment can be so decentralized. It is helpful to think about online audiences like distinct literate communities. Destiny’s debates with conservatives tends to attract conservative audiences to his community because Destiny is so successful at speaking their language. Conservatives love to talk about “debate,” and fantasize about destroying pink haired SJWs with facts and logic, only to be met with a man who will call you retarded in between multiple strong arguments about why diversity is what makes America the best country in the world. It is not wrong to say that this is the same type of disorientation you would experience if your parents started speaking coherent sentences about League of Legends game balance. Imagining the internet through literate communities can also explain why audience capture is such a danger. Changing the way you speak and behave can easily turn off the audience you have cultivated. For this reason, remember to guide your audience and not the other way around. Speak their language enough to convince them to join our community and platform.

## Always Shout Louder Than Your Accuser

​Marjane Satrapi is an Iranian author and illustrator, most well known for her graphic memoir Persepolis. She grew up during the Iranian Revolution in which she had to hide her true interests and personality from morality police. This gave her an incredible survivor strategy which can apply to present day American culture. In the context of her teacher accusing her of stealing jewelry, she writes that “I had learned that you should always shout louder than your accuser.”(9) If someone assumes that you have done something wrong, associate with immoral people, or hold unacceptable views, a grand display of public disapproval is a powerful tool for coercing you to change your behavior. This is why turnabout is a correct response. Karoline Leavitt at any press conference, relatives who bring up politics at Thanksgiving dinner, or friends who make quips about how “the radical left” does x or y are all practicing the politics of shame. By reacting in a calm manner and presenting facts in return, you only justify in their minds that you are an easy target. Do not let them accomplish this goal. Turn them into a target by responding loudly, emotionally, and with your best insults. Remember that Liberals have trained reactionaries into behaving this way because we always took the “they go low, we go high” approach. They are deeply unprepared for Liberals to start shaming those who deserve shame both loudly and aggressively.
