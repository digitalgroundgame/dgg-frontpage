# Climate, Environment, Renewable Energy, and Fossil Fuels

## Core principles

- The science is annoyingly simple:

  - Greenhouse gases cause us to retain more of the energy we get from the Sun, and it can be measured in a baffling number of different ways. At best, most of them rely on a middle-school understanding of chemistry to explain how taking a bajillion metric tonnes of carbon previously stored in the ground and putting it in the sky

  - Anyone showing you data to the contrary is cherry-picking either a location or a time period. Debunking almost any graph is ShrimpleAs "okay; can we check that against global data that includes the last century?"

- Always use proportional language:

  - We do not need to catastrophize; the truth is on our side.

  - Try to avoid ever using "absolute" vocabulary like always, never, forever, etc., unless the evidence justifies it (which is basically never, so maybe just avoid it altogether).

  - Stick to "data seems to suggest an increased risk for literally all of your unborn kin's lives to be 10-30% worse in xyz ways"-type beats. This both leaves you open to more data in the future and frames it as an inconvenience for people they (hopefully) care about. Or they are a sociopath and this is how you find out.

- Distinguish uncertainty from ignorance:

  - Scientific uncertainty reflects ranges, confidence bounds, and consideration for future studies. Models have limitations which need to be carefully understood to use properly; this is hardly a lack of knowledge.

  - Discover if you are engaging with an epistemic nihilist. Do they discredit almost any source of information?

- Provide an off-ramp:

  - Try to find a foundation of agreed-upon facts, and do what you can to build an argument from as many of those as you can. (this is anecdotal but I find it easier to help people reinterpret their facts than try and convince them a fact is actually true.)

  - Being wrong sucks; most people do not like it. If you truly want to have productive conversations, it requires you giving people the time and space to reflect, reorient, and reach new conclusions. This does not happen if you call them a dumbass for not falling to their knees and begging to be baptized in the name of Liberal Jesus after a 3 minute chat. The optimal strategy is to minimize defensiveness to maximize productivity.

  - That being said, not everyone is a good faith actor, and those conversations are categorically unproductive.

## When to disengage

If you notice one or more of the following behaviors:

- rejecting direct measurements from multiple independent sources (e.g., atmospheric CO₂ records, satellite temperature data),

- shifting claims repeatedly when evidence is addressed, without acknowledging corrections,

- arguments relying on premises which are internally contradictory or flat-out nonsense (e.g. different parts of the planet are cooling or warming based on vibes),

- claims rejecting basic physical principles (e.g., conservation of energy) without offering an alternative explanation,

Do not be afraid to walk away from the conversation, (if you care about the person) or start trolling (if you do not). These are the signs of someone acting in bad faith, and there is nothing to be gained from these conversations besides whatever amusement you can extract.

However, even for the best of us, these conversations will always be unsatisfactorily exhausting, so take care.

# Persuasion

## “The climate has always changed!”

- Yes, and while past [climate changes](https://science.nasa.gov/climate-change/what-is-climate-change/) were driven by factors like [orbital shifts](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/earth-science/milankovitch-orbital-cycles-and-their-role-in-earths-climate/) or [volcanic activity](https://climate.nasa.gov/faq/42/what-do-volcanoes-have-to-do-with-climate-change/) and unfolded over [thousands of years](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/paleoclimatology/paleo-perspectives/global-warming); the current warming is occurring over [decades](https://science.nasa.gov/climate-change/evidence/) and aligns with directly measured increases in [greenhouse gases](https://www.epa.gov/ghgemissions/overview-greenhouse-gases) from [fossil fuel](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-atmospheric-carbon-dioxide) [use](https://ourworldindata.org/fossil-fuels).

- Multiple independent observations show that recent warming is driven primarily by [human activity](https://climate.nasa.gov/causes/). These include [direct measurements of rising atmospheric CO₂ concentrations](https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/), [satellite observations showing Earth retaining more energy than it emits](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/features/EnergyBalance), and [attribution studies](https://climate.nasa.gov/evidence/) demonstrating that [natural drivers cannot explain observed warming trends](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-why-scientists-think-100-of-global-warming-is-due-to-humans/).

## “The science is not settled!”

- Scientific consensus means broad agreement across multiple independent lines of evidence, rather than unanimity or the absence of debate.

- Debate does indeed exist over impacts, timelines, and solutions, not [whether warming is occurring](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-global-temperature) or [what is causing it](https://www.epa.gov/climatechange-science/causes-climate-change).

- In science, disagreement over details is normal, but the core conclusion that human greenhouse gas emissions are causing global warming is supported by [decades of evidence](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-indicators) and accepted by the overwhelming majority of people who live anywhere near reality.

## “CO₂ is plant food, so warming is good!”

- The [CO₂ fertilization effect](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ab79e5) refers to increased plant growth under elevated CO₂ levels, while factors such as [nitrogen limitation](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/10/181022135722.htm), [extreme heat](https://www.cdc.gov/climate-health/php/resources/protect-yourself-from-the-dangers-of-extreme-heat.html), and [drought](https://cpo.noaa.gov/nca5-drought-and-climate-change-in-10-maps/) constrain real-world plant productivity.

- Research shows that while [elevated CO₂ can increase plant growth in controlled experiments](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j-2486.2006.01240.x), [heat extremes and water stress reduce crop yields](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-021-01000-1), and these risks increase with warming, which can offset or reverse any CO₂ fertilization benefits. Not to mention, this greening effect reversed a few decades ago.

## “It is real, but not dangerous; We will [adapt](https://youtu.be/0-w-pdqwiBw)!” ([lukewarmism](https://youtu.be/0AW4nSq0hAc))

- The Earth does have a degree(heh) of [climate adaptation](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/science-blog/climate-adaptation-and-what-means), however, [mitigation](https://www.epa.gov/enforcement/national-enforcement-and-compliance-initiative-mitigating-climate-change) is crucial in order to [secure the existence of our people and a future for our human children](https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/climate-impacts/climate-impacts-agriculture-and-food-supply_.html). Adaptation becomes harder, more expensive, and sometimes impossible as [warming increases](https://www.carbonbrief.org/explainer-how-climate-risks-increase-with-each-degree-of-warming/), which is why emissions reductions matter.

- Some impacts, like [sea-level rise](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-global-sea-level) or [ecosystem loss](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/climate-change-escalates-threats-species-spotlight), cannot be easily reversed. Delaying mitigation shifts larger costs and risks onto future generations and limits our ability to adapt effectively.

- Evidence shows that [adaptation limits are already being reached in some regions](https://www.carbonbrief.org/explainer-what-are-the-limits-to-climate-adaptation/), and that risks increase sharply with higher levels of warming.

## “Climate action costs more than doing nothing!”

- Mitigation costs are investments required to [reduce greenhouse gas emissions](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2023/executive-summary).

- The real comparison is the cost of action versus the rising costs of climate damage, which are already substantial and growing.

- [Extreme weather](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/billions/), [health impacts](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-and-health), and [infrastructure damage](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/04/04/climate-change-could-cost-us-2-trillion-each-year-by-2100-omb.html) already cost billions each year. Delaying action increases these damages and locks in higher long-term costs.

- Economic analyses show that [climate-related disasters already impose large and rising costs](https://www.munichre.com/en/company/media-relations/media-information-and-corporate-news/media-information/2026/natural-disaster-figures-2025.html), and that [mitigation reduces long-term economic risk](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05224-9).

- Public opinion

According to the [Yale Program on Climate Change Communication](https://climatecommunication.yale.edu/publications/climate-change-in-the-american-mind-politics-policy-spring-2025/), majorities of U.S. voters support clean energy development, pollution regulation, and climate education including substantial bipartisan agreement on many policies.

## Why Use Coal When Renewables Are More Cost Effective?

- Without even debating the reality of climate change, simply ask why we should build more coal power plants over onshore wind turbines or solar power farms.

- [Lazard (Investment Bank)’s 2025 report](https://www.lazard.com/news-announcements/lazard-releases-2025-levelized-cost-of-energyplus-report-pr/) on levelised cost of energy (LCOE) found renewable energy to be “the most competitive form of generation” [even without subsidies](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/wind-and-solar-energy-are-cheaper-than-electricity-from-fossil-fuel-plants/)

- “Onshore wind and utility-scale solar remain the most cost-effective forms of new-build energy generation on an unsubsidised basis.” - [Lazard, Key Findings from the 2025 LCOE+ Report , point 1. ](https://www.lazard.com/news-announcements/lazard-releases-2025-levelized-cost-of-energyplus-report-pr/)

- The cost of building new gas-fired energy plants has reached a 10 year high.

- New combined-cycle natural gas plants are more expensive, however existing gas plants (and maybe even coal plants.. Big maybe!) have lower LCOE’s.

  - If this is brought up, mention rising energy demand. More demand requires more infrastructure, Lazard has shown that building more renewable infrastructure is more efficient than building more gas plants.

  - Want to debate actual data? A simple data overview is provided in this [Scientific American article](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/wind-and-solar-energy-are-cheaper-than-electricity-from-fossil-fuel-plants/). Exact data is in the Lazard report.

  - Using LCOE, renewables remained the most cost-effective new energy generation.

  - [“91% of newly commissioned utility-scale capacity delivering power at a lower cost than the cheapest, newly installed fossil fuel-based alternative.”](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_Summary_2025.pdf)
