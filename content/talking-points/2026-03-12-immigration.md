## Why is this important?

Whether you agree or disagree, Immigration is one of the most important issues in the American consciousness right now. Your average American is convinced at this point of demanding a strong border policy, and perceived softness on the border is a deal-breaker in elections (even in states far away from the Southern border).

For the Right, immigration does not stop at being merely a policy issue but a part of their platform that fuels a larger historical narrative of decline, replacement, and mismanagement. At present, this narrative discredits the unprecedented victory of Liberalism across the world by portraying it as a “trade-off” of identity, coherency, and reality in exchange for material prosperity enjoyed by a relative few. This has become as close to a coherent tenet of the MAGA movement as there can be, and being equipped with the tools to combat this narrative comes closest to toppling larger belief structures and sowing doubt even among the hardcore of MAGA.

Liberals have been on the right side of Immigration for centuries in the United States. Democrats are today’s party of Liberalism, and thus the inheritors of any and all credit for the incredible prosperity and power that the United States has today due to our immigration policies in the past. Immigration drives economic growth, deepens our international relations with other countries, and serves as an important pillar of our moral leadership to the world. We not only have to connect the failures of Republicans to the American voter, but firmly defend ourselves from a media narrative decades old - claims that we are for “open borders” being only the tip of the iceberg.

# Persuasion

## Foreword:

Arguing as a Democrat can put you at a disadvantage due to biases and our [unpopularity on the issue](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/03/03/where-americans-stand-on-the-economy-immigration-and-other-issues-as-trump-addresses-congress/). To that end, it may serve you well to represent yourself first, and emphasize your Democratic Party or Candidate Name affiliation after you’ve made a strong, positive impression.

1. Avoid going to the other extreme - ensure the conversation ultimately reflects your decision to vote for Liberal Dems, so that good impressions support our political goals.

2. Strike a balance depending on the conversation. Use the talking points below as they resonate with both you and your conversation partner, particularly when highlighting Democratic priorities.

# Arguments:

## Political Principles

- At the start of any discussion, assess whether your conversation partner is likely to weigh political principles heavily in their view of immigration.

- Like any argument based on political principles, your conversation partner may decide that these considerations are insufficient or that immigration should be restricted. At that point, pivot to the following Economic Positives talking points.

## The only moral choice:

Only in the Democratic Party will you find a moral center on immigration. Democratic candidates, past and present, have promoted a balanced approach - [strengthening border security while remaining humane in their policies](https://stanton.house.gov/2025/8/new-dems-unveil-new-plan-to-secure-the-border-and-reform-the-immigration-system/). By contrast, many Republican politicians, especially today, have too often relied on performative cruelty (which they claim is necessary) to distract from their failure to seriously address the issue. In our present moment, the MAGA movement has effectively made an enemy out of successful immigrant integration into the country ([including refugees from Afghanistan](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/01/10/afghan-refugees-kansas-national-guard-shooting/)) the closer you can tie an example to the local community, the better.

- Ask if they’ve seen or are aware of the harsh treatment carried out by ICE. If they seem reluctant, use factual annual deportation numbers to illustrate that Republican policies are not only cruel but also ineffective. (See: Debate)

- Highlight that if Republicans are willing to lie or misrepresent their own failures, they are equally willing to lie or misrepresent the Democratic Party’s actual successes.

## Land of Opportunity:

The United States has always been a beacon of hope and opportunity - a place where those willing to faithfully engage in the American enterprise are welcomed as equals. In being a Land of Opportunity, we must not discriminate against newcomers seeking to participate fully in our society. By doing so, we demonstrate a courage that many other nations lack, avoiding the trap of treating immigrants as second-class citizens or as mere guest laborers, effectively subjecting them to conditions akin to slavery.

- Tie this to your conversation partner personally: ask if they would be comfortable with their forebears being treated as little more than laborers in service of a country they or their descendants could never fully enjoy.

- Generally, educate yourself on the Asylum process beyond the imagined ‘immigrants are just coming in through the Southern border’. It's a generally good practice and is an easy way to have credibility when speaking to this issue.

Last Hope of Free People

America stands as a refuge for those fleeing persecution and tragedy. In the 1930s, [strict immigration codes](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/immigration-to-the-united-states-1933-41) turned away tens of thousands of Jewish hopefuls, leaving them to face the Nazis. Today, we face the same moral test with Sudanese, Ethiopian, Burmese, and Ukrainian asylum seekers: [we save who we can](https://theburgnews.com/around-the-burg/harrisburg-area-refugees-share-their-stories-perspectives-amidst-shifting-immigration-landscape), rather than casting them back to a place where they expect their own slaughter.

- Make a point to emphasize the individuality of each immigrant, don’t accept a frame that accepts all refugees / immigrants as the same. This article I’ve linked does a great job of that.

- Use historical examples like the Holocaust carefully - gauge whether this will resonate with your conversation partner. If it may derail or alienate them, skip it.

## The American Advantage

Assimilation in America does not mean abandoning one’s heritage; it means contributing to the American story while retaining your identity. Throughout history, immigrants and marginalized communities have enriched our nation in ways that others could not. Consider [the Navajo Codetalkers](https://www.gilderlehrman.org/history-resources/essays/indigenous-americans-world-war-ii-navajo-code-talkers) of World War II: by using their native language, they created an unbreakable code that helped secure victory over the Fascist empires, demonstrating how cultural distinctiveness can serve the American enterprise in a way these more repressive regimes simply could not.

- Highlight to your conversation partner that assimilation can be a source of national strength, innovation, and resilience. Emphasize that newcomers contribute without erasing who they are, making America uniquely dynamic and capable of meeting challenges.

- Connect to modern immigration: just as past communities contributed in extraordinary ways, today’s immigrants bring skills, perspectives, and energy that enrich the nation.

- Find historical stories that have a way of escaping our modern partisanship.

## The American Way:

The idea of assimilation, or the meaningful acceptance of the American way of life, need not come with expectations of cultural erasure. In generations past, we did not require great lengths to adjoin oneself to the American society ([the Great American melting pot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ZQl6XBo64M)) and we quickly expanded ourselves from a culture predominantly English-Protestant into a much broader cross-section of the world’s cultures, with little issues today from the integration of so many nations’ cultures. As a newcomer you should not be expected to abandon your cultural heritage, and to threaten otherwise actually sours the opinion of immigrant communities towards America as a whole.

- Connect with your conversation partner on their own culture and what their family sacrificed in coming to the United States. Ask whether there are parts of their heritage they might be disallowed from rediscovering because they differ from the “American way of life,” emphasizing how unreasonable that would be.

# Economic Benefits

## Brain Gain:

Some of the greatest innovations in the world happened right on America’s shores - because America welcomed the talent to make them happen. A fair immigration system allows the United States to attract the brightest minds in the world - people whose ideas, inventions, and innovations flourish more in the United States than anywhere else. Albert Einstein, Nikola Tesla, and Elon Musk are just a few examples of immigrants whose contributions transformed science, technology, and industry in America by leaps and bounds. By welcoming talent, we ensure that innovation in the world is happening here on U.S. soil, strengthening our economy, our global leadership, and everyday life for all Americans.

- In conversation, point out that many things your partner uses or values were invented or improved by immigrants. Once they acknowledge this, connect it to how Liberal policies have historically made the U.S. a hub for global talent.

- Contrast this with Republican policy: travel bans, strict quotas, and a culture of fear actively discourage talented immigrants, leaving only those forced by extreme necessity rather than attracted by opportunity.

## Mature Investments:

Immigrants arriving as adults bring immediate value to the United States. Unlike children, whose upbringing requires decades of investment in healthcare, education, and childcare, adult immigrants are already trained, educated, and ready to contribute to the workforce. They pay taxes, start businesses, and strengthen social and economic networks without the government having to invest in their foundational development. [Even their children’s costs are offset by these contributions](https://forumtogether.org/article/immigrants-as-economic-contributors-immigrant-tax-contributions-and-spending-power/), creating a net benefit for communities and the country.

- In conversation, use this proactively or in response to concerns about “immigrants leeching off the system.” Emphasize that adult immigrants are not a drain, and are instead pivotal to our nation’s meteoric economic growth.

- Highlight examples: skilled workers filling critical shortages, entrepreneurs starting companies, or experienced professionals enhancing innovation in medicine, engineering, and technology.

# Debate

## Cruelty, not Enforcement

> “Immigration enforcement is inherently coercive; deterrence only works if consequences are severe and visible.”

Or: Democrats are characterized as being naive, or alternatively hostile to law enforcement.

Your response should do two things:

1. Point out that cruelty does not deter evenly - [it only deters immigrants coming here by choice.](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/blog/immigrants-coming-to-the-border-deterrence-policies/) The harsher the system, the more it pushes away immigrants with resources, education, and lawful alternatives, while doing far less to stop those with no other refuge. In practice, this selects against the very immigrants the United States claims to want: those most likely to comply with the law, integrate quickly, and contribute economically[1].

## Sovereignty demands Lawfulness

> “We can’t have a lawless country.”

Or: Democrats have a larger image in the public imagination of being hostile to law enforcement.

You cannot possibly dismantle this image in a single conversation, but you can know this and prepare to present a challenge to their worldview in a few ways:

1. Lead by affirming support for legitimate, lawful enforcement, and - where appropriate - ground that support in personal belief or experience. Go on to draw a clear distinction between respect for lawful authority and concern over the legal grey zone in which ICE frequently operates, an intentional grey area that Republicans have failed to clarify through statute or regulation. When enforcement lacks clear legal boundaries, it ceases to be about lawfulness and becomes arbitrary power - something that every American should oppose.

2. Emphasize that ICE enforcement has repeatedly harmed lawful permanent residents and U.S. citizens through [misidentification and overreach](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/deportation-industrial-complex-arrests-and-detentions-numbers). Even the most enforcement-friendly voters struggle to justify brutality when it is used against native-born Americans. A system that relies on cruelty undermines public trust and the legitimacy of the law itself.

## National Security ≠ Border Security

> “Uncontrolled borders is an unacceptable risk to National Security.”

Or: Democrats are often accused of wanting open borders, whereby any threat to our national security can cross over.

You cannot win by minimizing authentic concerns for increased security, instead you choose to reframe what effective security looks like:

1. Acknowledge that border management matters, but distinguish between theatrical border enforcement and genuine national security. Most of the great evils (terrorism, human trafficking, and smuggling) Republicans seem to demand further action on, [come down to a lack of security at legal points of entry](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/border-security-and-comprehensive-immigration-reform/) rather than our heavily militarized southern border. The spectacle that is made of our Southern border and mass deportation [has drawn away resources that are better spent on hunting down these criminal networks](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/thousands-agents-diverted-trump-immigration-crackdown-2025-03-22/).

2. Furthermore, to accept the claim that all most of the immigrant community in the United States are criminals that need to be hunted down with the power of the state creates the very conditions for [a regime of state terror](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/2000-federal-agents-sent-to-minneapolis-area-to-carry-out-largest-immigration-operation-ever-ice-says.) which will inevitably do more harm to Americans than anything currently justifying the ICE crackdowns.

## Immigration has nothing to do with Scarcity

> “Immigrants drive up retail prices, housing costs, and compete for American jobs.”

Or: Democrat policies that encourage immigration are blamed for the affordability crisis.

Scarcity, as an economic principle exists, but you would do well to recall our earlier argument and answer this directly:

1. The current affordability crisis is a result of policy failure and not immigration. Housing shortages, strained healthcare systems, and rising prices are overwhelmingly the result of zoning restrictions, underbuilding, supply-chain constraints, and market concentration - not population growth alone. Immigrants do not create scarcity in housing markets that refuse to build, or labor markets distorted by monopolistic power. Blaming immigrants substitutes a demographic explanation for a policy one and WILL NOT solve the real issue.

2. [Emphasize that immigrants expand supply as well as demand](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-immigration-means-for-u-s-employment-and-wages/). Immigrants are disproportionately represented in construction, healthcare, logistics, and service sectors - the very industries needed to relieve bottlenecks and lower costs.

## Welfare Contributors, not Leeches

> “Immigrants drain public resources and exploit the welfare state.”

Or: Democrats are accused of subsidizing dependency.

There is a fundamental misconception about immigrants and their contribution to the welfare state, for this we will borrow from our prior arguments to correct the underlying misunderstanding:

1. Most immigrants, especially undocumented immigrants, [are legally barred from accessing federal welfare benefits](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/sites/default/files/publications/mpi-immigrants-benefits-explainer-2024-final.pdf). The image of widespread benefit exploitation depends on ignoring all of this legal specificity. In reality, immigrants pay sales, payroll, and property taxes (directly or indirectly) while often being excluded from the very programs those taxes support.

## Democrats Are Not “Importing Votes”

> “Immigration is a political strategy to bring in new Democratic voters.”

Or: Republicans often frame Democrats as manipulating the electorate.

Challenge this oversimplification unapologetically:

1. Emphasize that naturalization and voter participation are complex, [slow processes which do not always lead to votes](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/09/19/1-in-10-eligible-voters-in-the-u-s-are-naturalized-citizens/?utm_source=chatgpt.com). Immigrants, especially those arriving recently, often take years or decades to gain citizenship and the ability to vote, and their political preferences are far from monolithic.

2. Nor is there a larger strategy among Democrats to bring in a “mass importation” of immigrants. Speak to your conversation partner on this as a Democrat yourself, emphasize that nothing you have seen supports this pure fantasy proposed by Republicans. For those more skeptical, pose the question: can ‘socialist democrats’ really seek to build support among staunchly anti-communist like Cubans or Venezuelans? Does our failure to do so change our attitude? We support each and every community that is in the country, regardless of and even in spite of their partisanship.

## Immigrants Don’t Erode Culture, They Bolster it

“High levels of immigration weaken national identity and shared values.”

Or: Republicans frame multiculturalism as a threat to cohesion.

1. [Cultural adaptation is bidirectional](https://doi.org/10.1177/21677026231159667). For what we take in as an influence, we push an influence many times larger; given that, what is more likely? That the American culture is fundamentally changed from what it was, or that the American culture takes root in these people who have already shown our values?

2. Point out that anecdotal or symbolic examples of cultural friction do not reflect larger trends. Crime or localized tension is often cited rhetorically, but comprehensive studies find that immigrants are [less likely to commit violent crime](https://www.cato.org/policy-analysis/immigrants-cut-victimization-rates-boost-crime-reporting) than native-born populations and add to a local community’s cohesion. Using culture as a scare tactic oversimplifies complex social dynamics and misrepresents the overall impact of immigration.

## Legal Immigration? Make the damn laws then!

> “I support legal immigration, not illegal immigration.”

Or: Democrats are said to be for open borders again, but this time it's framed as being opposed to order and fairness.

You can accept this distinction without any cost, and you can leverage this ‘concession’ to expose how selectively this is applied:

1. Republican support for legal immigration is all rhetoric and has no corresponding policy commitment. They oppose increases in legal immigration pathways which include family reunification, [refugee admissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAISE_Act), and [temporary work visas](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/14/immigrant-visas-suspended-trump). They are even going so far as to [roll back the legal status of hundreds of thousands of immigrants despite having had their day in court](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-administration-orders-review-biden-era-refugees-memo-shows-2025-11-24/).

2. Furthermore, their deployment of ICE [outside of federal courthouses](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/they-thought-they-were-in-court-for-a-routine-immigration-hearing-but-walked-into-a-deportation-trap/) has disrupted the legal process of seeking citizenship in the United States, even among those who are legal non-citizens. Current immigration enforcement officers make [no effective distinction in legal status](https://apnews.com/article/ice-arrests-warrants-minneapolis-trump-00d0ab0338e82341fd91b160758aeb2d).

3. Remind them that legality is a function of legal statute, not of morality. Many migrants are only classified as illegal because Congress has failed for decades to [update immigration law](https://www.cato.org/study/reforming-immigration-system-brief-outline) to reflect economic demand and humanitarian obligations. Our best angle from which to attack the Republican institutional advantage in the Senate for those competitive races in the upcoming electoral map. Democrats can’t possibly get anything done without both chambers!

## Sanctuary Cities are Federalism, not Federal Tyranny

> “Sanctuary cities are undermining federal law and protecting criminals.”

Or: Democrats are framed as sabotaging enforcement from the inside.

You are not going to win by arguing that local governments should ignore federal law, but you can clarify what sanctuary policies actually do:

1. Explain that [sanctuary policies](https://www.glovisa.org/sanctuary-cities/) don’t obstruct, and instead set jurisdictional limits for the Federal Government in Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Local and state governments are not required to enforce federal civil immigration law, and [MANY](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-publishes-list-sanctuary-jurisdictions) localities choose not to in order to preserve trust between police and communities. When immigrants fear that routine contact with local police will lead to deportation they refrain from reporting serious crimes and [public safety suffers as a result](https://www.thecgo.org/research/sanctuary-cities-and-crime/).

  - For this, DO NOT rely on my notes entirely. Do the research, get your facts straight. Any uncertainty in your delivery of this can damage the legitimacy of your entire argument and lose your single chance.

2. The alternative, to be clear, is that the whole government of the United States is ALL an extension of the Federal Government, and specifically the Executive Branch - [which under the Trump administration falls entirely under the authority of a single man in the President of the United States](https://apnews.com/article/trump-poll-presidential-power-executive-authority-d580838bfa4c88cbeadc204b7ddd184f).

3. Point out the selective federalism at work. Republicans often champion states’ rights, except apparently when local discretion interferes with immigration enforcement. Forcing local law enforcement to carry out federal civil detentions diverts resources from local priorities and exposes jurisdictions to legal liability. [Sanctuary policies simply insist that federal agencies do their own jobs](https://www.glovisa.org/sanctuary-cities/), within constitutional bounds that respect the local and state officials that make up this country.

## Chain Migration: Complete Families only bring Stability

> “By allowing chain migration, you open the door to unlimited immigration, a flood of people utilizing this loophole to immigrate to the country and escape deserved deportation.”

Or: Democrats’ most moralistic arguments are said to disguise schemes for unchecked immigration.

You can reject this argument outright with fact-based argumentation and hopefully damage your interlocutor’s faith in their prejudices:

1. Family-based immigration is not unlimited or automatic. It operates under[ strict numerical caps, long backlogs, and eligibility rules that already constrain volume](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R43145). Framing it as an uncontrolled “chain” obscures the reality that family reunification is one of the most heavily regulated components of the system.

2. Family ties are an integration asset, not a liability. Immigrants who arrive with familial support are more likely to find stable housing, secure employment, and integrate into their communities. [Family reunification reduces reliance on public assistance, increases labor force participation, and accelerates assimilation.](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2019/10/international-migration-outlook-2019_db218c95/c3e35eec-en.pdf)
